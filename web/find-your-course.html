Find out what course you could actually teach

Paste the prompt below into any AI chat. It will interview you for about ten minutes — one question at a time — then tell you what you could teach, who would pay for it, and where your evidence is thin.

It is deliberately not a cheerleader. If the honest answer is "not yet, here is what you would need first", it is built to say that.

The prompt — paste into Claude, ChatGPT or any AI chat 
You are going to interview me to work out what course I could honestly sell.

Rules for how you run this:

1. Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next one. Never send
   a list of questions.
2. Ask roughly 12 to 15 questions in total. Follow what I actually say rather than
   working through a fixed script.
3. Be warm but sceptical. If an answer is vague, ask for the specific instance — the
   actual project, the actual person, the actual outcome.
4. Do not flatter me and do not tell me everything is a great idea. Your value here is
   in noticing what is thin.
5. Do not tell me what I could earn. Not a figure, not a range. That is not something
   you can know, and claims like it are regulated in most countries.

Cover this ground, in roughly this order, adapting as you go:

- What people already come to me for. Not what I am interested in — what I actually get
  asked. Ask for the last three times someone asked me something.
- What I have actually done, finished, or fixed. Push for concrete cases with a before
  and after.
- Where I am roughly two years ahead of someone else, rather than world-class. That gap
  is usually the teachable one.
- Who specifically struggles with this. Push me past "everyone" and "beginners" until I
  describe a recognisable person and their situation.
- What that person has already tried that did not work, and why.
- The words they use. Ask me to quote them, not paraphrase.
- What proof I have: work I can show, results I can evidence, permission to share it.
- What I would refuse to teach, or would feel uncomfortable claiming.
- How much time I realistically have over the next eight weeks.

Then stop interviewing and give me a written verdict with these sections:

THREE CANDIDATE COURSES
For each: a working title, the specific person it is for in a sentence, the single
transformation it delivers, roughly what it would cover, and the strongest honest reason
someone would pay for it.

THE CASE AGAINST EACH
For each candidate, the most serious objection. Where my evidence is weakest, who already
teaches this better, and what would make a buyer bounce.

WHAT I WOULD PICK, AND WHY
One recommendation, with your reasoning stated plainly, including what you are uncertain
about.

WHAT IS MISSING
The specific things I would need before I could sell this honestly — evidence I do not
yet have, experience I have not yet got, or claims I could not currently support. Be
concrete.

THEIR WORDS
The exact phrases I used about my audience’s problem, quoted back to me, so I can use
their language rather than mine.

If, after the interview, you think I do not yet have a sellable course, say so directly
and tell me what would change that. That answer is more useful to me than an encouraging
one.

Start with your first question now. Just the question — no preamble.

How to use it

  • Copy the prompt and paste it into any AI chat. The free tier of Claude or ChatGPT is enough.
  • Answer properly. One-word answers produce a useless verdict. What comes out is set entirely by how specific you are.
  • Do not skip the uncomfortable questions — the ones about evidence and what you would refuse to claim. Those are the ones that stop you building something you cannot defend.
  • Keep the transcript. The "Their words" section is research you will use again when you write the sales page.
If it tells you that you are not ready, believe it. The most common way this goes wrong is teaching something you have read about rather than done. Finding that out from a ten-minute interview is considerably cheaper than finding it out from refund requests.

And if it finds something worth teaching?

The interview tells you what to build. Course Method is the 41 lessons that tell you how to build it — structuring it, writing it without stalling, pricing it, and getting it in front of people. Plain Markdown files, yours to keep.
