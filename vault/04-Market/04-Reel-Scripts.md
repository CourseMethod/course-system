# 4.4 — Reel scripts and formats

**11 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The shape of a short video

Four parts. 25–45 seconds total for most content.

```
0–3s    HOOK        Create the gap
3–8s    SETUP       Why this matters / what's wrong
8–30s   PAYOFF      The actual answer
30–40s  CLOSE       One next step
```

The proportions matter more than the exact seconds. **Most of the time is payoff.** The
common failure is spending twenty seconds on setup and five on the answer, which is
backwards and gets abandoned halfway.

---

## Deliver the payoff early

Counter-intuitive but reliable: **do not save the answer for the end.**

Holding it back ("stay tuned to find out") is a technique from television, where the
viewer had already committed. Here, people leave. Give the answer, then expand on it.

> **Weak:** "There are three things ruining your photos. Watch to the end for the most
> important one."
> **Strong:** "The main thing ruining your photos is the ceiling light. Here's why, and
> here's what to do instead."

The second holds attention better, because it has demonstrated value rather than promised
it.

## Five formats that work

### 1. The fix (most reliable)
```
HOOK    "Your product photos look grey. It's not the camera."
SETUP   "It's the ceiling light. It's orange, and your phone
         compensates by making everything else blue-grey."
PAYOFF  "Turn it off. Move within a metre of a window.
         That's it — same phone, same product."
         [show both photos side by side]
CLOSE   "More like this in my bio."
```

### 2. The mistake list
```
HOOK    "Four things making your photos look amateur. First one:"
PAYOFF  Four quick items, ~5 seconds each, each shown
CLOSE   "Which one are you doing?"
```
Comments on these are excellent, because people self-identify.

### 3. The before and after
```
HOOK    [First frame is the contrast] "Same candle. Ten seconds apart."
SETUP   "The only difference is where I stood."
PAYOFF  Show the change being made, in real time
CLOSE   "Takes ten seconds. Costs nothing."
```
Highest-retention format available if your topic is visual.

### 4. The confession
```
HOOK    "I spent £300 on a lighting kit. It's still in the box."
SETUP   "I thought I didn't have enough light. I had the wrong light."
PAYOFF  What actually solved it
CLOSE   "Save the £300."
```

### 5. The answered question
```
HOOK    "Someone asked why their photos look fine on their phone
         and terrible on the listing."
PAYOFF  The answer
CLOSE   "Send me yours if you want a look."
```
Invites DMs, which is where sales come from ([[06-Comments-And-DMs]]).

## Writing the script

**Write it out.** Do not improvise. Improvised video has filler — "um", "so basically",
"what I mean is" — and filler in the first three seconds is fatal.

**Aim for roughly 2.5 words per second.** A 35-second video is about 85 words. That is
short. It forces you to cut everything that is not the point, which is the discipline that
makes short-form work.

**Read it aloud and time it.** Always longer than you think.

**Cut every unnecessary word.** Specifically:

| Cut | Keep |
|-----|------|
| "So basically what you want to do is..." | "Do this:" |
| "One of the things that can really help is..." | "This helps:" |
| "I would definitely recommend that you..." | "Do this." |
| "At the end of the day..." | *(delete)* |

## Production, honestly

You need less than you think:

- **Your phone.** Genuinely. Modern phone cameras are excellent.
- **Daylight.** Free, and better than most equipment.
- **Quiet room** or cheap lapel mic. Audio matters more than video quality — people
  tolerate a soft image and abandon bad sound instantly.
- **CapCut** (free) for captions and cuts.

**Captions are not optional.** Most short-form is watched muted. CapCut auto-generates
them; check them for errors, because auto-captions mangle technical terms and a wrong word
on screen is distracting.

Do not buy equipment before post 50. You will not know what you need until then, and the
£300 lighting kit in the confession example above is a real pattern.

---

## Batching

Do not make one video per session. It is inefficient — most of the time goes on setup.

**Batch:** one afternoon, write ten scripts. Another, film all ten. Another, edit all ten.
That yields two weeks of content from three sessions.

Batching also protects consistency, which is the thing that actually matters
([[05-Content-Calendar]]). You will have weeks where you cannot film. A buffer means those
weeks do not become gaps.

---

## Reusing across platforms

Film once, post to Reels, TikTok and Shorts.

Two caveats worth knowing:

**Remove the watermark.** Platforms demote video with a competitor's watermark. Export
from CapCut without one rather than downloading from TikTok.

**Adjust the close.** "Link in bio" works on Instagram. TikTok and YouTube have different
conventions. Small edit, real difference.

---

## The close

One call to action. Not three.

| Goal | Close |
|------|-------|
| Build the list | "Free checklist — link in bio." |
| Start conversations | "Comment 'photos' and I'll send it." |
| Get engagement | "Which one are you doing?" |
| Sell | "Full course in bio." |

**Early on, prefer conversation and list-building over selling.** A comment or an email
address from someone who does not know you yet is worth more than a sales pitch they will
ignore. Sell to people who have seen you five times, not once.

---

## Action Items

1. **Write ten scripts** using your best hooks from [[03-Hooks]] and the five formats
   here. One sitting.
2. **Read each aloud and time it.** Cut to under 40 seconds.
3. **Strip the filler.** Use the cut/keep table.
4. **Film all ten in one session.** Do not perfect any of them.
5. **Add captions to every one.** Check for auto-caption errors.
6. **Pick one close per post**, weighted towards conversation and list-building.
