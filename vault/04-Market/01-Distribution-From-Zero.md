# 4.1 — Distribution from zero followers

**12 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The thing that changed

For most of the internet's history, reach required an audience. To get seen you needed
followers, and to get followers you needed to be seen. A closed loop, broken only by money
or luck.

Short-form video platforms broke it. TikTok, Reels and Shorts distribute primarily by
**content performance**, not follower count. A first post from an account with zero
followers can reach fifty thousand people.

That is the actual opportunity here, and it is genuinely new. It is also widely
misunderstood, in a way that wastes months.

---

## What "the algorithm" is actually doing

Simplified, but accurate enough to act on:

1. Your post is shown to a small test group — a few hundred people
2. The platform measures how they respond
3. If the signals are good, it shows it to a larger group
4. Repeat

The signals, roughly in order of weight:

| Signal | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Watch time / completion** | Did they watch to the end? The dominant signal |
| **Rewatches** | Strongest positive signal available |
| **Shares** | Second strongest — someone vouched for it |
| **Saves** | They intend to return to it |
| **Comments** | Engagement, though volume matters less than people think |
| **Likes** | Weakest meaningful signal |

**Watch time dominates.** This has one enormous implication that determines almost
everything about how you make content:

> **The first three seconds decide whether anything else matters.**

If people scroll past in two seconds, nothing else you did counts. That is why
[[03-Hooks]] exists as a whole lesson.

---

## Consequences worth internalising

### Followers are a lagging indicator, not an input

You do not need followers to get reach. You get followers *because* you got reach. Chasing
follower count directly — follow-for-follow, engagement pods — optimises the wrong thing
and produces an audience that does not buy.

### Every post is a fresh attempt

A post that flops costs you nothing. Your next one starts from the same place. This is
enormously freeing once you believe it: there is no penalty for a bad post, only for not
posting.

### Consistency beats quality, up to a point

Ten decent posts beat one excellent one, because you get ten attempts at the test group
and ten data points about what works. Perfectionism is expensive here specifically because
it reduces attempt count.

The floor still matters — genuinely bad content does not get picked up. But the marginal
value of the fourth hour of editing is far lower than the value of a second post.

---

## The realistic curve

| Posts | What typically happens |
|-------|------------------------|
| 1–10 | Nothing. 50–300 views. Feels pointless |
| 10–30 | Occasional 1–3k view post. First comments |
| 30–60 | One post does much better. You learn something |
| 60–100 | Patterns emerge. A format that works |
| 100+ | Compounding, if you kept going |

**Most people stop somewhere around post 12**, which is precisely the stretch where
nothing happens by design. Knowing the curve is what gets you past it.

---

## What actually gets you your first sales

Not the viral post. Almost nobody's first sale comes from a big view count.

It comes from **conversations**. Someone comments, you reply properly, they DM you, you
have an actual exchange, they buy. That is the shape of nearly every early sale in this
business.

This has a practical implication: **replying to comments is not a nice extra, it is the
main activity.** A post with 400 views and six real conversations is worth more at this
stage than one with 40,000 views and no replies. See [[06-Comments-And-DMs]].

---

## Where to put your effort

If you have five hours a week:

| Activity | Hours | Why |
|----------|-------|-----|
| Making posts | 2.5 | The raw material |
| Replying to comments and DMs | 1.5 | Where sales actually come from |
| Studying what worked | 0.5 | Your own analytics, not gurus |
| Email list | 0.5 | The asset you own ([[07-Email-List]]) |

Note what is not on the list: redesigning your profile, researching hashtag strategies,
watching videos about growth. Those feel like work and produce nothing.

---

## The one thing you own

Every platform can change its rules, throttle your reach, or ban you by mistake with no
appeal. Building entirely on rented land is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

**So convert reach into an email list from the beginning** ([[07-Email-List]]). The list
is yours. If every platform vanished tomorrow, you would still have a way to reach the
people who wanted to hear from you.

Start this on day one, not when you have "enough" followers.

---

## Action Items

1. **Accept the curve.** Write in `My Course.md`: *"I will post consistently for 60 posts
   before evaluating whether this works."* Deciding now, while calm, prevents deciding in
   week three while discouraged.
2. **Set your weekly hours** and split them using the table above. Actual calendar blocks.
3. **Start now**, before the course is finished. Everything you are writing is material.
4. **Set up your email capture** this week, even if it is a single form.
5. **Commit to replying** to every comment for the first 90 days. Every one.
