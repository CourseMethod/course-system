# 4.7 — Email list from day one

**10 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The only asset you actually own

Every platform can change its rules, throttle your reach overnight, or ban your account by
mistake with no meaningful appeal. This is not paranoia — it happens routinely, to people
who did nothing wrong.

**Your email list is yours.** If every platform vanished tomorrow, you could still reach
the people who wanted to hear from you.

That is the argument. It is enough on its own, and it is also true that email converts
several times better than social for actually selling things, because it arrives in a
place people check deliberately rather than scroll past.

---

## Start on day one

The common mistake: "I'll set up a list once I have enough followers."

There is no threshold. **Start with your first post.** Fifty engaged subscribers is worth
more than five thousand passive followers, and you will wish you had started earlier —
everyone does.

---

## What you need

Less than you think:

- **A form** people can fill in
- **Somewhere to store addresses**
- **A way to send to them**

**Free options that are genuinely fine to start:** MailerLite, Buttondown, Kit
(ConvertKit) free tiers. All handle a few hundred to a few thousand subscribers free, and
all let you export your list, which is the only feature that really matters.

Do not spend a week choosing. Pick one, use it, move later if needed. Exporting a list is
easy.

---

## The lead magnet

People do not give an email address for "updates". They give it for something specific and
immediately useful.

**What works:**

| Type | Why |
|------|-----|
| **A checklist** | Immediate, obviously useful, quick for you to make |
| **A template** | Removes a blank page |
| **A one-page cheat sheet** | Scannable, saveable |
| **The first lesson of your course** | Proves quality, and costs nothing |
| **A short email course** (3–5 emails) | Builds the habit of opening your email |

**What does not work:** "Join my newsletter", "Get updates", "Free ebook" with no
specifics, a 40-page PDF nobody reads.

**Make it genuinely small.** A one-page checklist gets used. A 40-page guide gets
downloaded and forgotten, and an unused lead magnet does not build the trust you collected
the address for.

You already have a good one: pull the checklist you made in
[[02-Build/08-Action-Items]], or your best single lesson.

---

## Where to ask

- **Bio link** — should go to your capture page, not straight to sales, early on
- **End of posts** — "free checklist, link in bio" as your close
  ([[04-Reel-Scripts]])
- **In replies** — "want the checklist? I'll send it"
- **On your sales page** — for people not ready to buy
- **After purchase** — customers are already on the list

---

## What to actually send

The thing that kills lists is not sending. You collect addresses, feel you have nothing
worth saying, wait, and by the time you email, nobody remembers signing up and they mark
it spam.

**Email at a rate you can sustain. Weekly or fortnightly. Then keep to it.**

What to send:

- **One useful thing.** A tip, a fix, a short lesson. Standalone value.
- **Something you learned** this week.
- **A question**, and read the answers. Replies are the strongest deliverability signal
  there is — they teach mail providers your address is wanted.
- **Occasionally**, something you are selling. One in five at most.

Same ratio as social: mostly useful, occasionally selling.

---

## Writing them

- **Plain text, or nearly.** Heavily designed emails look like marketing and get filed as
  such. A plain email from a person gets read.
- **Short.** 200–400 words is plenty.
- **One idea per email.**
- **Reply-to a real inbox.** A `no-reply@` address tells people you do not want to hear
  from them.
- **Subject lines that describe**, not tease. "The ceiling light problem" beats "You won't
  believe this simple trick".

---

## The legal bits, briefly

Not optional, and not complicated:

- **Consent.** They must actively opt in. No pre-ticked boxes, no adding people who
  emailed you once.
- **Unsubscribe link** in every marketing email. Honour it immediately.
- **Your identity and a postal address** in the footer — required under CAN-SPAM in the
  US and equivalents elsewhere. A PO box is fine.
- **Say what they are signing up for** at the point of signup.
- **GDPR**, if you have EU/UK subscribers: record when and how they consented. Your email
  provider does this for you.

Every reputable provider handles most of this automatically. Do not hand-roll a mailing
list to avoid £10 a month.

---

## Segmenting, minimally

Two groups is enough to start:

- **Subscribers** — have not bought
- **Customers** — have bought

Send the sales emails to the first group. Send the course sequence
([[03-Sell/08-Email-Sequence]]) to the second. Sending an upgrade pitch to someone who
already bought the top tier looks like you are not paying attention.

That is all the segmentation you need for a long time.

---

## Action Items

1. **Pick a provider today.** MailerLite or Buttondown if undecided. Ten minutes.
2. **Make your lead magnet** from material you already have — a checklist or your best
   lesson. One page.
3. **Set up the form** and put the link in your bio.
4. **Write your welcome email** — deliver the magnet, say what they will get, how often.
5. **Set your sending rhythm** — weekly or fortnightly — and put it in your calendar.
6. **Check the legal basics**: unsubscribe link, identity, postal address, explicit
   consent.
