# 4.2 — Choosing your platform

**10 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## Pick one

The instinct is to be everywhere. Resist it.

Each platform has its own format conventions, its own audience expectations and its own
rhythm. Learning one properly takes a couple of months. Spreading five hours a week across
four platforms means being mediocre on all of them and learning nothing from any.

**Pick one. Get good. Add a second only when the first is working.**

Repurposing is fine once you have a system — the same vertical video posts to Reels,
TikTok and Shorts with minimal effort. But *learn* on one.

---

## How to choose

Two questions, in this order.

### 1. Where is your audience actually?

Not where you like being. Where they are.

| Your audience | Likely platform |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Small business owners, trades, local services | Facebook, Instagram |
| Creative, visual, hobby, lifestyle | Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok |
| Under 30, broad consumer | TikTok |
| Professional, B2B, career | LinkedIn |
| Technical, niche, research-minded | YouTube, Reddit, X |
| Anything people search for | YouTube, and a blog |
| Crafts, home, planning, food | Pinterest — consistently underrated |

Test it: search your topic on two candidate platforms. Where is there active, recent
discussion with real engagement? That is where your people are.

### 2. What can you actually sustain?

Be honest about this, because it determines whether you last.

- Hate being on camera? → faceless formats: screen recordings, text-on-screen, voiceover.
  Works well on TikTok, Reels and Shorts.
- Better at writing than talking? → LinkedIn, X, Reddit, a blog.
- Comfortable talking at length? → YouTube long-form, which has the best search longevity
  of anything.
- Have visual work to show? → Instagram, Pinterest.

**The platform you will still be posting on in three months beats the theoretically
optimal one.**

## The realistic comparison

| Platform | Reach from zero | Effort per post | Longevity | Sells well? |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **TikTok** | Highest | Low–medium | Days | Moderate — young, price-sensitive |
| **Instagram Reels** | High | Low–medium | Days | Good — strong DM culture |
| **YouTube Shorts** | High | Low–medium | Weeks | Moderate — feeds long-form |
| **YouTube long-form** | Slow to start | High | **Years** | **Best** — high trust |
| **LinkedIn** | Good in B2B | Low (text) | Days | Good for professional topics |
| **Pinterest** | Moderate | Low | **Months** | Good for visual/how-to |
| **X** | Low without following | Very low | Hours | Poor alone |
| **Reddit** | High if genuine | Low | Weeks | Good indirectly, hostile to selling |

Two entries worth noticing because people overlook them:

**YouTube long-form** has the worst start and the best end state. A video that ranks
brings viewers for years. If you can sustain the production effort, it compounds better
than anything else here.

**Pinterest** is persistently underrated for how-to topics. It is a search engine that
people forget is a search engine, pins surface for months, and competition is far lower
than on video platforms.

---

## The faceless question

You do not need to show your face. Formats that work without it:

- **Screen recording with voiceover** — excellent for anything software-based
- **Text-on-screen over B-roll** — very low effort, works well
- **Hands only** — for anything physical or craft
- **Whiteboard or paper** — surprisingly effective
- **Slides with narration**

The honest caveat: **showing your face does build trust faster.** People buy from people,
and a face shortens that. It is a trade, not a free choice, and pretending otherwise would
be doing you a disservice.

Plenty of people run this entirely faceless. It is somewhat slower to build the same
trust, and it is perfectly viable.

## Setting up properly

Ten minutes, once:

- **Bio: who you help and with what.** Not "entrepreneur | dreamer | coffee". Something
  like "Product photos for Etsy sellers. Phone + window, no studio."
- **One link**, going to your email capture or sales page.
- **Profile picture** that is recognisable at 40px.
- **Username** that is findable and consistent across platforms.
- **Switch to a creator/business account** so you get analytics. You need them for
  [[08-Reading-Analytics]].

Do not spend an afternoon here. It is the least important work in this module and the most
tempting, because it feels productive and has a clear finish line.

---

## Reddit, specifically

Worth its own note because it is high-value and easy to get wrong.

Reddit will give you real reach if you genuinely participate, and will remove you fast if
you show up to promote. The rule: **answer questions properly, for weeks, before
mentioning anything you sell.** Read each subreddit's rules on self-promotion, because
they vary and they are enforced.

Done right it is one of the best places to find your first customers, because people are
there asking the exact question your course answers.

---

## Action Items

1. **Pick one platform.** Write it in `My Course.md` with the reason.
2. **Check your audience is there:** search your topic, look for recent active discussion.
3. **Decide your format** — face or faceless — and commit, so you stop reconsidering it.
4. **Set up the profile properly.** Ten minutes, then stop.
5. **Find three accounts** doing something similar well. Note their format, not their
   topic.
6. **Commit to 60 posts** on this one platform before adding another.
