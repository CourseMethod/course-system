# 4.9 — What to do when nothing works

**12 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## Read this before you need it

You will hit a point where you have done the work, followed the process, and nothing is
happening. No views, no sales, no sign that anyone cares.

This lesson exists so that when you get there, you have a diagnostic procedure instead of
a decision made while discouraged. Decisions made in that state are almost always "quit",
and it is almost always too early.

---

## First: is anything actually wrong?

Before diagnosing, check you are past the point where nothing happening is *expected*:

| Posts made | Sales | Verdict |
|------------|-------|---------|
| Under 20 | 0 | **Normal.** Keep going. Nothing to diagnose |
| 20–40 | 0 | **Normal.** Still early |
| 40–60 | 0 | Worth checking the funnel, keep posting |
| 60+ | 0 | **Now diagnose properly** |
| 60+ | 1–5 | It works. Needs volume |

If you are under 40 posts, close this lesson and go and make another post. There is
nothing to fix yet, and looking for a problem will find one that does not exist.

---

## The diagnostic ladder

Work top to bottom. **Stop at the first failure and fix only that** — this is the
important part. Changing five things at once means you learn nothing from the result.

### Step 1 — Are people seeing the posts?

**Check:** views per post over the last twenty.

Consistently under 300? People are not seeing it. The cause is almost always retention at
three seconds ([[03-Hooks]]).

**Fix:** write 30 new hooks. Post ten with dramatically stronger openings. Measure
3-second retention, not views.

**Do not** change your topic, your niche or your product at this step. The problem is the
first three seconds.

### Step 2 — Are they watching?

**Check:** 3-second retention and average watch percentage.

- Low 3-second retention → hook problem, back to step 1.
- Good 3-second, low average → the post does not deliver on the hook. Too much setup,
  payoff too late ([[04-Reel-Scripts]]).

### Step 3 — Are they clicking?

**Check:** link clicks against views.

Good views, no clicks. Two causes:

**Wrong audience.** Your content travels but attracts people with no interest in the
outcome. Common with entertainment-shaped posts.
**Fix:** make content closer to the problem your course solves. More specific, narrower,
less broadly appealing. Fewer views, better viewers.

**No bridge.** You never connect the content to the thing. Your close is missing or weak.
**Fix:** end every post with one clear next step ([[04-Reel-Scripts]]).

### Step 4 — Are they buying?

**Check:** conversion rate in `/admin.html`. Needs 200+ visitors to mean anything.

Under 1% with real traffic is a page problem. In rough order of likelihood:

1. **Not clear what it is.** A stranger cannot tell in ten seconds.
2. **Not clear who it is for.** No disqualification section
   ([[03-Sell/03-The-Sales-Page]]).
3. **Not enough specifics.** Contents hidden, vague claims.
4. **No risk reversal.** Guarantee missing or conditional.
5. **Price mismatch.** Rare, and people check it first because it is the easiest thing to
   change. Check it last.

**Fix one at a time**, wait 200 visitors, measure.

### Step 5 — Are they finishing checkout?

**Check:** checkout abandonment in the dashboard.

High abandonment — people click buy and do not complete — usually means a technical
problem, not a persuasion one. Test the whole flow yourself on a phone. Buy your own
course.

---

## The uncomfortable possibility

If you get through all five steps and everything checks out, consider that **nobody wants
this specific thing.**

Not "you cannot do this". Not "the idea is bad". This specific topic, framed this way, for
this audience.

Signs it is genuinely this:

- Content gets decent reach but people never ask about the outcome
- Comments are about the entertainment, never the problem
- Nobody has ever asked you for help with this unprompted
- Competing products do not seem to sell either

**What to do:** do not throw it away. Re-aim it.

The common fix is narrowing. "Photography course" → "Product photos for Etsy sellers".
Same material, specific buyer. This works far more often than starting over, because the
problem is usually the framing, not the content.

You now have a written course, a working sales system, an audience and a distribution
habit. Re-aiming costs you a fortnight. Starting again costs you everything.

---

## The real reason people stop

Not any of the above. It is that the work stops feeling worth it when there is no signal.

Three things that help, honestly:

**1. Change what you are measuring.** Early on, measure *inputs*, not outputs. "Did I post
five times?" is in your control. "Did I get sales?" is not, yet. Track the thing you can
do.

**2. Get one real conversation.** One person telling you a post helped them will sustain
you for a fortnight. Go and have conversations ([[06-Comments-And-DMs]]) — they are
available to you today, and sales are not.

**3. Remember the curve.** Most people stop around post 12. Being at post 45 with nothing
to show means you are further than almost everyone. The distribution of outcomes here is
very heavily determined by who kept going.

---

## When quitting is the right call

It genuinely sometimes is, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest:

- You have done 60+ posts, been through the ladder, re-aimed once, and there is still no
  signal.
- You have discovered you do not want to write about this, at all.
- Your circumstances changed and the time is not there.

If so, stop cleanly rather than guiltily. Keep the course file — it costs nothing to leave
a page up. You have learned writing, selling, and distribution, and those transfer
completely to whatever is next.

**That is not failure. It is an experiment that gave you a clear answer for about a
hundred hours and fifteen pounds.**

---

## Action Items

1. **Check the "is anything wrong?" table first.** Under 40 posts, stop reading and go
   post.
2. **Work the ladder in order.** Write down where it first fails.
3. **Change one thing.** Wait for a real sample. Measure.
4. **Have three real conversations** this week regardless of numbers.
5. **Switch to measuring inputs** — posts made, conversations had — until outputs appear.
6. **If you have been through all five steps and re-aimed once**, give yourself permission
   to decide either way, deliberately rather than by drifting.
