# 4.5 — The 30-day posting calendar

**10 minutes · Module 4 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## Why a calendar rather than inspiration

Because inspiration produces bursts, and bursts do not work.

The pattern without a plan: enthusiastic week of daily posting, a week of nothing, a
guilty post, two weeks of nothing, quietly stop. Everyone does this. A calendar is the
only reliable defence.

**Consistency beats volume.** Four posts a week for three months beats fourteen posts in
week one and nothing after.

---

## Pick a rate you can sustain in a bad week

Not your good week. Your bad week — the one with a work deadline and a sick child.

| Rate | Realistic if | Growth |
|------|--------------|--------|
| 3/week | You have 2–3 hours | Slow but real |
| 5/week | You have 4–5 hours | Solid |
| 7/week | You batch well, 6+ hours | Fast |
| 14/week | Full-time on this | Fast, high burnout risk |

**Five a week is the sweet spot** for most people doing this alongside a job. Enough to
learn quickly, sustainable for months.

Whatever you pick, **you are committing for 60 posts.** At five a week that is twelve
weeks. Put the end date in your calendar.

---

## The content mix

Post types, in rough proportion:

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Teach one thing** | 50% | The core. One tip, one fix, one mistake |
| **Show the problem** | 20% | Before/after, what wrong looks like |
| **Personal** | 15% | Your mistakes, your process. Builds trust |
| **Answer a question** | 10% | From comments, DMs, Reddit |
| **Mention the product** | 5% | Directly, no more than this |

**That last row is deliberately small.** One in twenty posts selling. Everything else is
useful on its own terms.

The reason is not modesty. An account that mostly sells gets unfollowed and stops being
distributed. An account that mostly teaches builds an audience that buys when you do ask.
Your product is mentioned in the bio on all 20 posts anyway.

---

## Where the 30 ideas come from

You already have them. You wrote a course.

**Every lesson is at least one post.** A 20-lesson course is 20 posts before you have
thought about it. A lesson with four sub-points may be four.

Sources, in order of ease:

1. **Your lesson titles** — [[02-Build/03-Outlining]]
2. **Your action items** — each one is a "do this today" post
3. **Your examples and mistakes** — [[02-Build/07-Examples-And-Cases]]
4. **Cut material in `Course Two.md`** — [[01-Mindset/08-Committing-To-Scope]]
5. **`Their Words.md`** — every question is a post
6. **Your own support inbox**, once you have one

Sit down with these and you will have 40 ideas in half an hour.

## The calendar itself

A table in `Content-Calendar.md`. Fill in a fortnight ahead.

| Day | Hook | Format | Type | Status |
|-----|------|--------|------|--------|
| Mon 1 | "Your photos look grey. Not the camera." | Fix | Teach | Filmed |
| Tue 2 | "Same candle, ten seconds apart." | Before/after | Problem | Scripted |
| Wed 3 | "£300 lighting kit. Still in the box." | Confession | Personal | Idea |
| Thu 4 | "Four things that look amateur. First:" | List | Teach | Idea |
| Fri 5 | "Someone asked why theirs look fine on the phone" | Question | Answer | Idea |

Five columns, no more. A calendar that takes effort to maintain gets abandoned.

## The weekly rhythm

One planning session, one batch session, daily posting. About four hours:

**Sunday, 60 min** — review last week's numbers ([[08-Reading-Analytics]]), fill the next
week's calendar, write five scripts.

**Monday, 90 min** — film all five. Edit all five. Schedule them.

**Daily, 20 min** — post (or let the scheduler post), then **reply to every comment**.

That daily twenty minutes is not optional. It is where the sales come from
([[06-Comments-And-DMs]]).

---

## Build a buffer

Aim to be **one week ahead** at all times.

Life interrupts. Without a buffer an interruption becomes a gap, a gap becomes two, and
two gaps is usually where people stop. With a buffer, a bad week costs you nothing
visible.

Getting ahead is the hardest part and it is worth one painful weekend early on.

---

## Timing and hashtags

Two things people spend far too much energy on.

**Posting time** matters much less than it used to. Short-form platforms distribute over
days, not minutes. Pick a time you can hit consistently and stop optimising it.

**Hashtags** matter less than almost anyone claims. Three to five relevant ones is plenty.
Thirty hashtags does not help and looks desperate. The content decides the reach.

Time spent on either of these is time not spent making a post, and the post is what
matters.

---

## Tracking

At the end of each week, note for each post: views, retention at three seconds, comments,
saves.

You are looking for **patterns, not individual results**. One post doing well is noise.
Four confession-format posts outperforming everything else is a signal, and it tells you
what to make more of.

---

## Action Items

1. **Pick your rate** based on a bad week, not a good one. Write it down.
2. **Generate 40 ideas** from your course material in one sitting.
3. **Build the calendar table** and fill in the next two weeks.
4. **Schedule your two weekly sessions** as real calendar blocks.
5. **Batch your first ten posts** this weekend, to start with a buffer.
6. **Set your end date** — 60 posts from now — and commit to not evaluating before then.
