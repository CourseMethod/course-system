# 3.4 — Writing copy that isn't hype

**14 minutes · Module 3 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The reader's starting position

Assume this, because it is true: **your reader has seen a hundred pages like yours and
believes approximately none of them.**

They have a highly developed detector for a specific pattern — big promise, invented
urgency, five-star quotes from people who may not exist, a story about a life-changing
realisation. Every element of that pattern you use confirms their suspicion. Every
verifiable specific you offer disconfirms it.

This is why honesty outperforms hype here. Not because it is nicer — because the hype
register has been burned through by everyone who came before you, and credibility is now
the scarce resource.

---

## The core move: specificity

Vague claims are free, so they carry no information. Specific ones are costly to fake, so
they carry a lot.

| Hype | Specific |
|------|----------|
| "Transform your business" | "Stop losing two hours a week to invoicing" |
| "Proven system" | "The exact sequence I used, including the two steps I'd skip now" |
| "Thousands of students" | "40 lessons, 22,000 words, written over four months" |
| "Industry-leading results" | "My photos went from 3 sales a week to 11. Screenshot below." |
| "Unlock your potential" | "You'll be able to reshoot a 20-product range in one afternoon" |

The right column is more persuasive *and* easier to write, because you are describing
rather than inventing.

**The test:** could a competitor selling something worse write your sentence? If yes, it
is not doing any work.

---

## Say the thing they are thinking

Pre-empting the objection is more persuasive than answering it after it forms.

> "This is a course about selling courses, which is exactly the thing you should be
> suspicious of. Here's the distinction that matters..."

> "I don't have testimonials yet. I could write some. I'd rather tell you what I actually
> have instead."

> "You could find most of this free, in pieces, across about forty sources over three
> months. What you're buying is the order."

Every one of those sentences costs you something, and every one buys more than it costs.
Naming the objection yourself proves you are not hiding it, which is the thing the reader
is scanning for.

---

## Admit the limits

The strongest trust signal available to you is **telling people not to buy**.

- "This doesn't fit if you need money this month."
- "For your first few sales, Gumroad is genuinely the better choice. Start there."
- "You do not need this. Everything it automates can be done by hand."

This feels like leaving money on the table. In practice it converts the right people
harder — because someone who reads a genuine disqualification and buys anyway has
convinced themselves, which is far more durable than being convinced by you.

It also lowers your refund rate, which is worth real money ([[09-Refunds-And-Support]]).

---

## Write like a person

Read every sentence aloud. If you would not say it to someone in a pub, rewrite it.

| Advert voice | Person voice |
|--------------|--------------|
| "Unlock the secrets to..." | "Here's how it actually works." |
| "This proven methodology..." | "This is what I did." |
| "Are you tired of struggling with...?" | "Your photos look grey. Here's why." |
| "Imagine finally being able to..." | "You'll be able to do this in an afternoon." |
| "Don't miss out!" | "Launch price ends Friday, then it's £79." |

Specific tics to cut:

- **Rhetorical questions in a row.** "Tired of X? Frustrated by Y? Ready for Z?" — this
  is the most recognisable advert rhythm there is.
- **"Imagine..."** — asking the reader to fantasise is a manipulation they can feel.
- **Exclamation marks.** Almost never earn their place.
- **ALL CAPS** for emphasis.
- **"Secret", "hack", "loophole", "they don't want you to know".**
- **Fake intimacy.** "Hey friend" to a stranger.

---

## Structure sentences for reading on a phone

Most of your traffic is on a phone, holding it one-handed, half-distracted.

- **Short paragraphs.** Two to four sentences.
- **Short sentences,** varied. Long ones are fine when they carry a single idea, but a
  paragraph of them is a wall.
- **Bold the load-bearing sentence,** one or two per section. A reader who reads only the
  bold should get the argument.
- **Break up lists** with bullets. Five items in prose is hard; five bullets is easy.

---

## The specific case of AI-written copy

Do not ship it. See [[02-Build/06-Using-AI-Properly]] for the general argument; the sales
page case is sharper.

Sales copy is precisely where the generic register is most damaging, because the whole
job of the page is proving you are not generic. A page that reads as generated
communicates "this person did not care enough to write it", which is a reasonable
inference.

Use AI to find objections you missed and to tighten what you wrote. Write the words
yourself.

---

## Before and after

**Before:**

> Unlock the secrets of professional product photography! This comprehensive,
> step-by-step system reveals the proven methods used by industry leaders. Whether you're
> a complete beginner or looking to level up, this course has everything you need to
> transform your listings and skyrocket your sales. Don't miss out — join hundreds of
> satisfied students today!

**After:**

> Your products look better in person than in your photos. That's fixable, and it's
> almost never the camera.
>
> Twenty lessons on shooting listing photos with a phone and a window. You'll learn why
> your current photos look grey, how to fix it in about ten minutes, and how to shoot a
> whole range so it looks like a range.
>
> It won't help if you're photographing flat artwork — different problem, and I'll point
> you elsewhere for that.

The second is shorter, tells you more, and is far more believable. It also contains a
disqualification, which is the sentence doing the most work.

---

## Action Items

1. **Run the competitor test.** Every claim on your page: could someone selling something
   worse write this? Delete or specify anything that passes.
2. **Write three disqualifications.** Real ones. Put them on the page.
3. **Name your biggest objection** in your own words, before the reader gets to it.
4. **Read the whole page aloud.** Mark every sentence you would not say out loud, and
   rewrite those.
5. **Search for the tics**: exclamation marks, "imagine", "unlock", "secret", rhetorical
   question runs. Remove them.
