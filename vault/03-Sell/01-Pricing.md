# 3.1 — Pricing: the actual framework

**14 minutes · Module 3 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The mistake almost everyone makes

Pricing by effort. *"It took me forty hours, so..."*

Your production cost is irrelevant to the buyer. They are not buying your hours — they
have no idea how many there were and would not care. They are buying an outcome, and what
they will pay is a function of **what that outcome is worth to them** and **what else
they could do instead**.

A forty-hour course that saves someone a weekend is worth less than a four-hour course
that saves them £2,000.

---

## The three numbers that actually set your price

### 1. What is the alternative worth?

What would the buyer otherwise do, and what does that cost?

- Figure it out alone: three months of evenings
- Hire someone: £500–2,000
- A competing course: £30–200
- Do nothing and keep the problem: whatever the problem costs them

Your price lives between "meaningfully cheaper than the alternatives" and "expensive
enough to be taken seriously".

### 2. What does the outcome save or earn?

If your course reliably saves a small business owner ten hours a month, the outcome is
worth hundreds of pounds a year to them. £47 is not expensive against that.

If your course helps someone enjoy a hobby slightly more, the outcome is real but not
financial, and the price ceiling is much lower. Both are fine businesses; they are
different price points.

### 3. What does the price signal?

This is the counter-intuitive one and it is well documented: **too cheap reduces sales.**

A £7 course on a serious professional topic reads as low quality. The buyer does not think
"what a bargain" — they think "what is wrong with it". Price is one of the few quality
signals available before purchase.

---

## The bands

| Price | What it signals | Good for | Watch out for |
|-------|-----------------|----------|---------------|
| £5–19 | Impulse, low stakes | Very narrow tools, checklists | Reads as low value; needs high volume |
| **£29–69** | **Real product, easy decision** | **Most first courses** | **The sweet spot** |
| £79–149 | Serious, considered purchase | Professional outcomes, substantial courses | Needs stronger proof |
| £150–499 | Investment | Business outcomes, includes your time | Longer decision, more objections |
| £500+ | Transformation | Cohorts, coaching, direct access | Not a first product |

**Start at £29–69 for your first course.** Specifically, £47 or £49 is a well-trodden
price for good reasons: it is above the "what's wrong with it" threshold, below the point
where people need to think for a week, and it only takes a handful of sales to prove the
concept.

---

## Why raising the price later is easy and lowering it is not

Start lower than you think, then raise it. The asymmetry:

**Raising a price is easy.** Existing customers already bought at the old price and are
unaffected. New visitors have no idea it changed. You simply charge more.

**Lowering a price is expensive.** Existing customers who paid more feel cheated, and
some will ask for the difference. It also signals that the product was not selling, which
is visible to anyone watching.

A sensible ladder:

- **Launch:** £47
- **After ~25 sales:** £59 — you now have evidence people buy
- **After ~75 sales:** £79 — you have testimonials and material improvements
- **After ~150 sales:** £97+ — you have a track record

Each increase is justified by the product genuinely being better: more lessons, real
testimonials, refinements from customer feedback.

---

## Price endings

The research here is less decisive than marketing folklore claims, but the conventions:

- **£47, £49, £97** — the standard for digital products. Reads as considered.
- **£50, £100** — reads as round and slightly premium. Fine for higher tiers.
- **£47.99** — do not. It reads as retail and slightly cheap.

Do not agonise over this. The difference between £47 and £49 is not what determines
whether your business works.

---

## Currency

Charge in the currency of the majority of your audience, and just pick one. Stripe handles
international cards without you doing anything.

Do not try to price differently by region on a first product. It is complex, it is
gameable with a VPN, and it will consume a week you do not have.

---

## What about "value-based pricing" at £2,000?

You will see advice to charge far more, on the basis that a transformation is worth
thousands.

It can be true, and it requires things you do not have yet: substantial proof, a sales
conversation or long-form funnel, and usually your personal time included. A £2,000
product with no testimonials and no track record does not sell — it just gets no buyers
while you conclude the market is wrong.

Earn your way up. The ladder above gets you there faster than starting at the top and
stalling.

---

## Discounting

Be careful. A course that is permanently 60% off is not a £97 course, it is a £39 course
with a misleading sticker — and a struck-through price that was never actually charged is
a fictitious pricing claim, which is a real regulatory problem in most jurisdictions.

**Legitimate discounts:**
- A genuine launch price that genuinely rises on a stated date ([[05-Honest-Urgency]])
- A real, time-limited sale that actually ends
- A specific audience discount you honour (students, existing customers)

**Not legitimate:**
- A permanent "was £197, now £47" where nobody has ever paid £197
- A countdown that resets on refresh

The backend in this system will only display a struck-through price when a real launch
window is configured and the higher price genuinely takes effect afterwards. That is
deliberate — it makes the dishonest version take extra effort.

---

## Action Items

1. **Write down the three numbers**: cost of the alternative, value of the outcome, and
   what your price signals. In `My Course.md`.
2. **Pick your launch price.** £29–69 unless you have a specific reason otherwise.
3. **Write your price ladder** — the sales thresholds at which you will raise it. Decide
   now, while you are not anxious about sales.
4. **Write one sentence justifying your price** to a sceptical buyer. If you cannot, you
   do not believe it yet — work out why.
5. **Check your discount plan** against the legitimate/not list. If it fails, fix it now.
