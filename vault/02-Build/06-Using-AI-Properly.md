# 2.6 — Using AI without sounding like AI

**14 minutes · Module 2 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The honest position

AI is genuinely useful for building a course. It is also the fastest available way to
produce something worthless that people will recognise as worthless within a paragraph.

The difference is entirely in what you ask it to do.

**Use it for structure, editing and pressure-testing. Do not use it to write your
lessons.** The reasons are practical rather than moral, and they are worth understanding
rather than just following.

---

## Why AI-written lessons fail

**1. It does not know what you know.** Your entire competitive advantage is the specific
thing you learned the hard way — the setting that was not documented, the approach
everyone recommends that does not work, the mistake you made in month two. A model has
the average of the internet, which is precisely the generic material your reader has
already found and been unsatisfied by.

**2. It is recognisable.** Readers have developed a good ear for it. Certain rhythms,
certain vocabulary, an even texture with no sharp edges, a tendency to enumerate three
balanced considerations for everything. Once a reader suspects the course was generated,
every other quality signal stops mattering.

**3. It is confidently wrong in ways you may not catch.** In your own field you would spot
an error. In the adjacent areas your course touches, you might not — and a plausible
wrong detail in a paid product is worse than an omission.

**4. It removes the thing that makes it yours.** If the course could have been generated
from the title, there is no reason to buy it from you.

---

## What it is genuinely excellent at

### Outlining and structure

> *"I'm writing a course teaching [audience] to [outcome]. I know [list what you know].
> What order should these go in? What am I likely to be assuming they already know?"*

That second question is the valuable one. It catches dependency gaps
([[03-Outlining]]) that you cannot see because you know everything.

### Being the confused beginner

> *"Here is a lesson I wrote. You are [your specific reader from
> [[01-Mindset/04-Finding-Who-Its-For]]]. Tell me every point where you would be confused,
> and every term I used without explaining."*

Extremely effective. It will not be as good as a real reader, and it is available at
11pm and does not get bored.

### Editing what you wrote

> *"Tighten this without changing my voice or removing any specific detail. Cut hedging
> and throat-clearing. Do not add anything."*

The "do not add anything" is essential. Without it you get your paragraph plus a summary
paragraph plus a transitional sentence you did not want.

### Generating counter-arguments

> *"What are the three strongest objections to the advice in this lesson? Where is it
> wrong or oversimplified?"*

This makes lessons better. Addressing an objection you had not thought of is a real
quality improvement, and it is the kind of thinking that is tedious to do alone.

### First-pass sales copy, for you to rewrite

Useful as a starting point to react against. Never ship it — see
[[03-Sell/04-Writing-Copy]] for why AI sales copy in particular reads as untrustworthy.

---

## The workflow that works

**Write badly yourself → have AI find the problems → fix them yourself.**

Not: *AI drafts → you edit*. That order produces generic material with your edits sprinkled
on, and it retains the texture that readers recognise.

The order matters because the specific, hard-won, slightly odd details — the ones that
make the course worth money — only survive if they were there from the start.

---

## Prompts worth keeping

Put these in `Templates/AI-Prompts.md`:

**Structure check**
> Here is my course outline: [paste]. My reader is [description]. Where does this skip a
> step? What am I assuming they already know? What is out of order?

**Confused-beginner pass**
> You are [reader description]. Read this lesson and tell me: every term used without
> explanation, every point you would get stuck, every place you would ask "but how?".
> Do not rewrite it.

**Tighten**
> Tighten this. Keep my voice and every specific detail. Cut hedging, throat-clearing and
> restatement. Do not add sentences. Do not add a conclusion.

**Objections**
> What are the strongest objections to this lesson's advice? Where is it oversimplified
> or wrong? Be specific and blunt.

**Examples prompt** *(note the framing — it asks for prompts, not examples)*
> I need a concrete example for this point: [point]. Ask me five questions about my own
> experience that would surface a good one.

**Action items**
> Suggest three completable actions a reader could do in under 20 minutes after this
> lesson. They must produce something, not just "reflect".

---

## The tells to edit out

If you use AI for editing, watch for these creeping in:

- **"Delve", "unpack", "leverage", "robust", "landscape", "realm", "testament to"**
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** and **"This isn't about X. It's about Y."** — a rhythm
  that shows up constantly
- **Three-item lists everywhere**, all the same length
- **Em-dashes used as a verbal tic** rather than for genuine asides
- **A summary paragraph** at the end of every section
- **Balanced hedging** — "while there are benefits, there are also considerations"
- **No sharp edges.** Real writing has opinions and picks sides

The strongest signal of human writing is **specificity**: a real number, a real date, a
real thing that went wrong. Models produce plausible generality. You have actual detail.
Use it.

---

## Where AI genuinely should not go

- **Anything factual you cannot verify yourself**
- **Testimonials or reviews** — see [[01-Mindset/06-Ethics-And-Claims]]; generating these
  is illegal, not merely tacky
- **Case studies with numbers** — invented figures presented as real are fabrication
- **Your personal story.** It is the one part nobody else could write
- **Anything in a regulated area** where being confidently wrong has consequences

---

## Cost

Roughly £15–20/month for a paid tier of any major assistant, while you are building. You
do not need it, and it saves real time on structure and editing.

Free tiers are fine for the prompts above if the cost matters. This is not a tool that
determines whether you succeed.

---

## Action Items

1. **Create `Templates/AI-Prompts.md`** and paste the six prompts in.
2. **Run the structure check** on your outline from [[03-Outlining]]. Fix any dependency
   gap it finds.
3. **Run the confused-beginner pass** on your one finished lesson. Fix what it flags.
4. **Search your drafts** for the tell words listed above. Replace each with something
   specific.
5. **Write down the rule** in `My Course.md`: *I write the lessons. AI finds the problems.*
