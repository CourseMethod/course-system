# Testimonial request templates

The three permissions, kept separate:

1. **Permission to receive feedback** — they replied
2. **Permission to publish it** — a separate, explicit yes, with the name you'd use
3. **Evidence for any claim in it** — you have seen something

Conflating these is how sellers end up publishing quotes they were never given permission
to use. Full context: [[05-Bonus/04-Collecting-Testimonials]].

---

## 1. The request

Ask openly, including for the bad answers. You want the negative feedback — it is how the
product improves, and asking only for praise means you learn nothing.

```
Subject: Two minutes: how did it go?

Hi [NAME],

You bought [COURSE] a while back. I'd like to know what actually
happened — good, bad or nothing at all. All three are useful and the
last one is the most useful.

[LINK TO FORM]

Two minutes. Three questions, none compulsory.

ON PUBLISHING: the form asks separately whether I may quote you
publicly. If you say no, the feedback is still valuable and nothing
goes anywhere. If you say yes, I'll send you the exact wording and the
name I'd use, and it doesn't go live until you confirm.

Or just reply to this if that's easier.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 2. The form questions

Three questions that produce usable answers, rather than "great course, thanks".

```
1. What nearly stopped you buying? What happened instead?

2. What's the one thing you'd tell someone considering it?

3. What did you do differently after? (Numbers welcome but not
   required.)

---

[ ] You may quote me publicly on the sales page.
    (Unticked by default. You'll be sent the exact wording to
    confirm before anything goes live.)

Name you'd like used: ____________
```

Question 1 is the most valuable. **A customer answering the objection that nearly stopped
them buying** is more persuasive than anything you could write.

---

## 3. Confirming the wording

Not merely polite — this is your record that they agreed to *this* text under *this* name.

```
Hi [NAME],

Thank you for this — genuinely useful.

Would you be happy with this on the page?

    "[EXACT QUOTE AS IT WOULD APPEAR]"
    — [NAME], [SHORT DESCRIPTION]

Say no, or change any of it — no problem at all either way, and it
doesn't go anywhere until you say yes.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 4. Asking for evidence of a number

The framing matters. This lands well and is entirely true.

```
Hi [NAME],

One more thing — you mentioned [SPECIFIC NUMBER]. Would you mind
sending a screenshot?

Not that I doubt you at all. It's that if I put a figure on my sales
page, I have to be able to back it up if anyone asks.

If you'd rather not, completely fine — I'll use the quote without the
number.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

If they decline: **use the quote without the number.** Do not publish it anyway.

---

## 5. When they say no to publishing

```
Hi [NAME],

Understood, and thank you for the feedback regardless — it's the part
I actually act on.

Nothing goes anywhere.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

Keep the record. Do not ask again.

## What makes a testimonial worth publishing

| Weak — persuades nobody | Strong |
|--------------------------|--------|
| "Amazing course!" | "I'd been putting off [THING] for a year. Did all of it in an afternoon." |
| "So much value" | "The bit about [SPECIFIC] was the thing I'd never seen anywhere else." |
| "Highly recommend" | "I almost didn't buy because I thought I needed [THING]. You don't." |

Specific, and addressing a real hesitation. Anyone could have written the left column,
which is exactly why it persuades nobody.

---

## Display rules

- Real name and real description, as agreed
- Do not imply a result is typical when it is not
- "Results not typical" does **not** cure an unrepresentative claim
- Do not show positive while suppressing negative
- Only mark "verified" if you have actually seen evidence

---

## If you have none yet

Use the honest empty state on your page ([[07-Sales-Page-Skeleton]], section 9). It
converts better than invented quotes, because the reader believes it.

**Do not put placeholders up "temporarily".** That is precisely the illegal thing, and
"I was going to replace them" is not a defence.
