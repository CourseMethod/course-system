# Email sequence templates

Six emails, in sending order. Full reasoning in [[03-Sell/08-Email-Sequence]].

Replace `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`. Rewrite in your voice — a template used verbatim reads as a
template.

---

## 1. Delivery — send immediately

**The most important email you will ever send.** If it fails or lands in spam, the
customer's experience is "I paid and got nothing", and that becomes a refund within the
hour.

Plain subject, link first, no upsell, nothing that trips a promotional filter.

```
Subject: [COURSE NAME] — your download link

Your copy of [COURSE NAME] is ready.

Payment went through. Here's everything you just bought:

[DOWNLOAD LINK]

Save the file somewhere you'll find it again. This link works until
[DATE]. After that, or if you change computers, just reply to this
email and I'll send a fresh one — no hassle, no time limit on that.

WHAT TO DO NEXT (about 8 minutes)

1. Unzip the file
2. Install Obsidian — obsidian.md, free
3. Choose "Open folder as vault", point it at the unzipped folder
4. Open 00-Command-Center.md. It tells you where to start.

Prefer a plain text editor? That works too — every lesson is a
Markdown file. Obsidian just adds the links between them.

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

Reply to this email. It reaches me at [YOUR EMAIL] and I answer every
one within 24 hours, usually faster. Broken link, wrong file, changed
your mind — all fine, just say so.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 2. Welcome — 15 minutes later

Job: prevent overwhelm. Give them **one specific thing to do today.**

```
Subject: Before you start: the one mistake to avoid

Hi [FIRST NAME],

You now have [COURSE NAME]. [N] lessons is a lot to look at, so let me
save you the mistake almost everyone makes.

Don't read it all first.

The people who finish aren't the ones who read fastest. They're the
ones who read one lesson, do the thing at the bottom, then read the
next. People who binge the whole thing over a weekend feel productive
and have built nothing by Monday.

YOUR FIRST WEEK, HONESTLY

Module 1 — about [X] minutes, and by the end you've [OUTCOME]
Module 2 — three or four sittings, and by the end you have [OUTCOME]

That's it. Two modules. Don't touch [LATER MODULE] until [CONDITION].

ONE THING TO DO TODAY

Open [SPECIFIC LESSON FILE] and do the exercise in it. Takes [X]
minutes, and it's the decision everything else depends on.

If you get properly stuck, reply to this. I'd much rather answer a
small question this week than hear in a month that you never started.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 3. Orientation — day 2

Pure friction removal. The four questions everyone asks.

```
Subject: Setting up (4 common questions)

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Quick practical one. Most of the setup questions I get are the same
four, so here they are answered before you hit them.

"OBSIDIAN LOOKS COMPLICATED"
It's a folder of text files with a nice reader on top. You need three
things: click a link to follow it, Ctrl/Cmd+O to jump to any lesson by
name, and the left sidebar for structure. Ignore everything else.

"DO I HAVE TO USE OBSIDIAN?"
No. Every lesson is a plain .md file — VS Code, Notion, Typora, even
Notepad. You only lose the clickable links.

"WHERE DO I START?"
00-Command-Center.md. It's the map. If you feel lost, go back to it.

"MY LINK EXPIRED"
Reply and I'll send a new one. No limit, no charge — you bought it,
it's yours.

A tip almost nobody uses: make a note called "My [TOPIC].md" and dump
every idea into it as you read. By the end of Module 2 that scratch
file has become your outline.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 4. Check-in — day 7

**The highest-leverage email in the sequence.** Give three easy replies, one of which is
permission to admit they haven't started.

```
Subject: How's it going?

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Genuine question, and a short reply is a perfect reply. Which are you?

- "Building" — great. Reply with what you're working on, I'm curious.
- "Stuck on something" — reply with what. Specific questions get
  specific answers.
- "Haven't opened it" — reply with just that. No judgement, and I have
  a suggestion that helps.

That third one is why I send this. About a third of people who buy a
course never open it, and it's almost never about the course — it's
that the first step felt bigger than the twenty minutes they had free
that evening. If that's you, say so and I'll give you the twenty-
minute version.

THE BIT THAT TRIPS MOST PEOPLE

[The single most common sticking point in YOUR course, named
 specifically, with the resolution in 3-4 sentences.]

Reply and tell me where you are.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 5. What others built — day 21

**Only send the version with quotes if you have real, consented testimonials.** If you
don't, send the "what are you building?" version below and collect some.

See [[05-Bonus/04-Collecting-Testimonials]].

```
Subject: What are you building?

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Three weeks in. I'm collecting results from people working through
this, and I'd like yours — whatever stage it's at.

Reply with any of these:

- What you decided to [DO THE THING], and why that one
- What's working — a post that landed, a first sale, an email that
  got a reply
- What isn't. This is the more useful answer and it's the one I act on.

If something's worked and you're willing to let me quote you on it,
say so explicitly and I'll send you the exact wording to confirm
before it goes anywhere. I don't publish anything without that.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 6. Upgrade — day 30, lower tier only

Segment this. Sending it to someone who bought the top tier looks careless.

The fourth section — explicitly saying they don't need it — is what makes the rest
credible.

```
Subject: [THE PART AFTER X]

Hi [FIRST NAME],

A month in. If you've [DONE THE FIRST THING], there's a specific wall
you're about to hit, and it's not a motivation problem — it's a
plumbing problem.

[NAME THE WALL SPECIFICALLY — 3-4 sentences on what goes wrong and
 why people lose weeks there.]

WHAT [UPGRADE NAME] IS

- [Concrete thing]
- [Concrete thing]
- [Concrete thing]
- [Your time, if included]

UPGRADE PRICE: [DIFFERENCE ONLY]
You only pay the difference — what you already paid comes off.

[LINK]

And to be straight with you: you don't need this. [EXPLAIN THE FREE OR
CHEAPER ALTERNATIVE HONESTLY.] For [SITUATION] that's genuinely the
sensible choice. This is for when you want [THE ACTUAL REASON].

Either way, reply if you want a second opinion on which makes sense.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

## 7. Feedback request — day 45

Ask for the feedback and for permission to publish **as two separate questions**.

```
Subject: Two minutes: how did it go?

Hi [FIRST NAME],

You bought this a while back. I'd like to know what actually happened
— good, bad or nothing at all. All three are useful and the last one
is the most useful.

[LINK TO FORM]

Two minutes. Three questions, none compulsory.

ON PUBLISHING: the form asks separately whether I may quote you
publicly. If you say no, the feedback is still valuable and nothing
goes anywhere. If you say yes, I'll send you the exact wording and the
name I'd use, and it doesn't go live until you confirm. If you mention
numbers I'll ask for a screenshot before publishing — not because I
doubt you, but because if I put a figure on a sales page I have to be
able to stand behind it.

Or just reply to this if that's easier.

— [YOUR NAME]
```
