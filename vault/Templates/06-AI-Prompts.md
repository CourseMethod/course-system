# AI prompts

The rule, from [[02-Build/06-Using-AI-Properly]]:

> **You write the lessons. AI finds the problems.**

Not the other way round. AI-drafted-then-edited produces generic material with your edits
sprinkled on, and readers recognise it. The specific, hard-won details that make a course
worth money only survive if they were there from the start.

---

## Structure and outlining

**Dependency check** — catches gaps you cannot see because you know everything.

```
Here is my course outline:

[PASTE]

My reader is: [SPECIFIC READER FROM LESSON 1.4]

Where does this skip a step? What am I assuming they already know?
What is out of order? Do not rewrite it — just list the problems.
```

**Ordering**

```
I'm teaching [AUDIENCE] to [OUTCOME]. I know how to explain:
[LIST WHAT YOU KNOW]

What order should these go in, working backwards from the outcome?
What's missing between them?
```

## Improving what you wrote

**Confused-beginner pass** — the most useful prompt here.

```
You are [SPECIFIC READER DESCRIPTION].

Read this lesson and tell me:
- Every term I used without explaining it
- Every point where you'd get stuck
- Every place you'd ask "but how?"

Do not rewrite it. Just list them.

[PASTE LESSON]
```

**Tighten** — note the constraints; without them you get additions you did not want.

```
Tighten this. Keep my voice and every specific detail.
Cut hedging, throat-clearing and restatement.
Do not add sentences. Do not add a conclusion. Do not add transitions.

[PASTE]
```

**Objections**

```
What are the strongest objections to the advice in this lesson?
Where is it oversimplified or wrong? Be specific and blunt.

[PASTE]
```

**Examples** — note it asks for *questions*, not examples. An AI-invented example is
generic; your own is the product.

```
I need a concrete example for this point: [POINT]

Ask me five questions about my own experience that would surface a
good one. Don't write the example yourself.
```

**Action items**

```
Suggest three actions a reader could complete in under 20 minutes
after this lesson. Each must produce something — a file, a photo, a
list, a written decision. Not "reflect on" or "consider".

[PASTE LESSON]
```

## Sales page

Use these to react against. **Never ship AI-written sales copy** — it is precisely where
the generic register does most damage ([[03-Sell/04-Writing-Copy]]).

**Objection finder**

```
Here's my sales page. I'm selling [PRODUCT] to [AUDIENCE] for [PRICE].

What objections would a sceptical reader have that this page doesn't
answer? What would make them close the tab?

[PASTE]
```

**Specificity audit**

```
Go through this page. For every claim, tell me whether a competitor
selling something worse could write the same sentence.

List the ones that could be written by anyone.

[PASTE]
```

That second one is the single most useful sales-copy prompt here. Anything that survives
it is doing real work.

## Editing checks

**Find the AI tells** — useful even on your own writing, since these creep in.

```
Find every instance in this text of:
- "delve", "unpack", "leverage", "robust", "landscape", "realm",
  "testament to"
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- Three-item lists where all three are the same length
- Summary paragraphs at the end of sections
- Balanced hedging ("while there are benefits, there are also...")

List them with line context. Don't rewrite.

[PASTE]
```

**Read-aloud check**

```
Which sentences here would sound unnatural said out loud to a friend?
List them. Don't rewrite.

[PASTE]
```

## What never to use AI for

- **Testimonials or reviews** — generating these is illegal, not merely tacky
  ([[05-Bonus/04-Collecting-Testimonials]])
- **Case studies with numbers** — invented figures presented as real are fabrication
- **Anything factual you cannot verify yourself**
- **Your personal story** — the one part nobody else could write
- **Anything in a regulated area** where being confidently wrong has consequences
