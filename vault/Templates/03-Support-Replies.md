# Support reply templates

Four questions will be most of your support volume. Keep these to hand — not to be
impersonal, but to be fast and consistent. Speed is the thing customers actually notice.

Full context: [[03-Sell/09-Refunds-And-Support]].

---

## 1. "I didn't get the email"

The most common ticket. Answer within hours, not days — an unanswered one becomes a
chargeback.

```
Hi [NAME],

Sorry about that. Here's a fresh download link:

[LINK]

It's worth checking spam and the promotions tab too — that's where it
is about nine times out of ten.

If it's still not arriving, tell me and I'll send the file directly as
an attachment.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 2. "My link expired"

Never make this feel like their fault. It isn't.

```
Hi [NAME],

No problem at all — here's a new one:

[LINK]

Links expire after [X] hours for security, but there's no limit on
replacements and no charge. You bought it, it's yours. Just ask any
time.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 3. "How do I open this?"

```
Hi [NAME],

Straightforward:

1. Unzip the file you downloaded
2. Install Obsidian — obsidian.md, free
3. Open Obsidian, choose "Open folder as vault", point it at the
   unzipped folder
4. Open 00-Command-Center.md — it tells you where to start

If you'd rather not install anything, every lesson is a plain text
file. Open the folder and read them in whatever you like — you only
lose the clickable links between lessons.

Stuck at any step, tell me which and I'll walk you through it.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 4. Refund request

**Just do it.** No retention attempt, no "are you sure?". A difficult refund becomes a
chargeback, which costs you the sale price plus a ~£15 fee plus account risk.

```
Hi [NAME],

Refunded — it'll be back on your card in 5-10 days (your bank's
timing, not mine).

No hard feelings at all. Keep the vault if it's any use to you.

If there was something specific that didn't work, I'd genuinely like
to know — one line is plenty, and it's how this gets better. But no
obligation.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

Ask for the reason **after** refunding, never before. Before, it's an obstacle. After,
it's a genuine question and people often answer usefully.

---

## 5. "Is this right for me?" — pre-purchase

Answer honestly, including when the honest answer loses the sale. A wrong-fit customer
costs you a refund, a support thread, and sometimes a bad review.

```
Hi [NAME],

Honest answer: [YES / NO / DEPENDS].

It's a good fit if [SPECIFIC CONDITIONS].

It's not if [SPECIFIC CONDITIONS] — in that case [ALTERNATIVE] would
serve you better, and I'd rather point you there than take your money
for the wrong thing.

[If yes:] Happy to answer anything else before you decide.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 6. Angry or disappointed customer

Do not get defensive. Acknowledge, fix or refund, keep it short.

```
Hi [NAME],

You're right that [ACKNOWLEDGE THE SPECIFIC THING], and I'm sorry.

[EITHER: Here's the fix — ... ]
[OR: I've refunded you in full, it'll be back in 5-10 days.]

[If there's a genuine product problem:] This is useful and I'm
changing [SPECIFIC THING] because of it. Thank you for telling me
rather than just leaving.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

---

## 7. Technical problem you can't reproduce

```
Hi [NAME],

Thanks — I'd like to get to the bottom of this. Could you tell me:

- What you did just before it happened
- What you expected, and what happened instead
- Which browser/device
- A screenshot if you have one

In the meantime, here's [WORKAROUND OR DIRECT FILE] so you're not
stuck while I look into it.

— [YOUR NAME]
```

Note the last paragraph: **unblock them first, debug second.** A customer waiting on your
investigation is a customer considering a refund.

---

## The standard to hold

- **Reply within 24 hours.** That's the whole commitment. Not constant availability —
  reliability.
- **Unblock first, explain second.**
- **Never argue about a refund.**
- **Every repeated question is a documentation bug** — fix the FAQ, the README or the
  orientation email so the next person never asks.
