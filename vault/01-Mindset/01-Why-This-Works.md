# 1.1 — Why this works right now

**6 minutes · Module 1 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The uncomfortable version first

Let me start with what this is not, because the category has a credibility problem and
pretending otherwise wastes your time.

This is not a way to make money without working. It is not passive. It is not fast. The
people you have seen posting income screenshots are, in a meaningful fraction of cases,
making their money from teaching people to make money, which is a closed loop that
creates nothing. And a great many people who buy courses like this one never open them.

If that has made you suspicious of the whole idea, good. Hold onto that. It is the same
instinct your future customers will have about you, and understanding it is how you build
something they will actually trust.

Now the part that is true.

---

## The actual economics

Here is the thing that makes this work, stated plainly and without embellishment:

**Knowledge has a near-zero marginal cost and a non-zero price.**

If you spend forty hours writing a course, you have spent forty hours. Selling it to the
first person costs you nothing further. Selling it to the thousandth person also costs
you nothing further. The file does not wear out.

Compare that to how most people sell what they know:

| | Consulting / freelancing | A course |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to serve one more client | Your hours | ~zero |
| Ceiling | Hours in a week | None in practice |
| Income when you stop working | Zero | Continues |
| Effort profile | Constant | Front-loaded |

This is not a trick and it is not new. Textbooks have worked this way for four hundred
years. What has changed recently is that you no longer need a publisher's permission or
a distribution deal to participate.

---

## What actually changed

Three things, all within about the last decade:

**1. Distribution became free.** Short-form video reaches people who have never heard of
you at no cost. In 2010 reaching ten thousand strangers required an advertising budget.
Now it requires being interesting for thirty seconds. That is hard, but it is a different
kind of hard — one that effort can solve rather than capital.

**2. Payments became trivial.** Taking money online used to mean a merchant account, an
underwriting process and a lawyer. Now it is an afternoon with Stripe, and the platform
handles fraud, tax and compliance for a percentage.

**3. Writing tools got dramatically better.** Not because AI writes your course — it
should not, and Module 2 is explicit about why generic AI output is instantly detectable
and actively harmful to your credibility. But as a drafting aid, an editor and a
structuring tool, it removes a real chunk of the friction that used to stop people
finishing.

None of these is a secret. All three are available to everyone reading this, which is
exactly why execution rather than information is the scarce thing.

---

## Why "everyone is already doing this" is the wrong worry

The most common objection at this point is saturation. There are a lot of courses. There
are a lot of people teaching things.

Two reasons this matters less than it feels like it should.

**The market is not one market.** "Online courses" is not a category you compete in, any
more than "books" is. You compete in *how to do bookkeeping for small UK construction
firms*, or *conversational Portuguese for people who already speak Spanish*. Narrow those
down and the competition usually turns out to be one outdated blog post and a YouTube
video from 2019.

**Most of the competition is bad.** Not through malice — through generality. The vast
majority of courses in any niche are written at a level of abstraction that helps nobody:
"be consistent", "provide value", "know your audience". Specific beats good. If you write
down what you actually did, in the actual order, with the actual numbers and the actual
mistakes, you will be more useful than most of what exists.

---

## What this demands from you

Being honest about the trade you are making:

- **Front-loaded work.** Forty to eighty hours before you see a pound.
- **A tolerance for silence.** Your first posts will get nothing. This is universal and
  it is the point at which most people quit.
- **Willingness to write.** The writing is the product. If you will not write, none of
  the rest of this helps.
- **Patience measured in months.** Not days.

And what it does not demand: an audience, a face on camera, a credential, permission, or
capital beyond about £15 for a domain.

---

## The honest summary

You have knowledge that is worth something to someone slightly behind you. Packaging it
takes real work and the economics of digital goods mean that work compounds rather than
repeating. The tools to distribute and sell it are free or nearly so.

That is the whole opportunity. It is genuinely good. It is not easy, and anyone telling
you it is easy is the reason the category has a credibility problem.

---

## Action Items

1. **Write down the trade.** In `My Course.md`, answer honestly: *Am I prepared to spend
   40+ hours before earning anything, and to post for 30 days into silence?* If the
   answer is no, stop here — that is a legitimate answer and better discovered now.
2. **Name three narrow topics** you could plausibly teach. Not polished, just three
   phrases. You will refine them in the next lesson.
3. **Find your competition.** Search for each of the three. Note what already exists and
   how specific it is. Vague competition is an opportunity, not a warning.
