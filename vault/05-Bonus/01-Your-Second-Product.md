# 5.1 — Your second product

**11 minutes · Module 5 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## Why the second one is where the business is

Your first course cost you eighty hours and taught you the entire system — writing,
pricing, page, checkout, delivery, distribution.

**The second costs perhaps thirty, and sells to people who already trust you.**

That gap is the whole argument. Your existing customers are the warmest audience you will
ever have: they have paid you once, they know the quality, and reaching them costs one
email rather than months of posting.

Selling to an existing customer is dramatically cheaper than acquiring a new one. This is
not a subtle effect.

---

## When to start

**After roughly 50 sales of the first**, or once it sells steadily without daily
intervention.

Too early and you are splitting attention on a product that has not proven itself. Too
late and you leave the easiest revenue you will ever have on the table.

---

## What to build

In order of how well it usually works:

### 1. The obvious next step

What do your customers do *after* finishing? That is your second product, and your inbox
will tell you.

> Product photography → *Writing listings that convert*
> Git basics → *Git for teams*
> Running after 40 → *Your first half marathon*

Best option when it is available, because the demand is already demonstrated.

### 2. The thing they keep asking for

Read your support inbox. The question you have answered fifteen times is a product.

This is the most reliable signal in the entire business, and it costs nothing to collect —
it is already sitting in your email.

### 3. What you cut from the first

`Course Two.md`, from [[01-Mindset/08-Committing-To-Scope]]. You already did the thinking,
and you cut it for scope reasons, not quality ones.

### 4. The same thing for a different audience

Product photography for Etsy → for eBay resellers → for restaurant owners.

Cheapest option, because most of the material transfers. It is also the least
interesting to write, and it requires finding a new audience, which is the expensive part.

### 5. A deeper version

A premium tier of the same course, with your time attached. See
[[03-Sell/02-Tiers]] — it must contain something that genuinely costs you more.

---

## What not to build

**A course about how you built your course.** This is the loop from
[[01-Mindset/01-Why-This-Works]], and it is how good products turn into a pyramid of
people teaching each other to teach. Unless you have genuinely substantial results, do not.

**A subscription, yet.** Recurring revenue looks attractive and comes with a permanent
obligation: produce, forever, or churn destroys it. Consider it when you have evidence
people want continuous access, not before.

**A wildly different topic.** Your audience trusts you about one thing. Starting over in a
new field means starting over entirely, and you lose the advantage you just spent a year
building.

---

## The bundle

Once you have two products, sell them together at a small discount.

```
Course A            £47
Course B            £47
Both                £79
```

Some people take the bundle who would have bought one. Your average order value rises with
no additional work, and the bundle gives you a reason to email existing customers.

Do not discount so heavily that the individual price looks fictitious. 15–25% off the
combined price is plenty.

---

## Launching to your list

The advantage you have earned:

1. **Tell them it is coming**, two weeks out. Ask what they want in it — genuinely, and
   act on the answers.
2. **Offer it at a real discount to existing customers first.** Say why: they took a
   chance on you. Make the discount meaningful and make it end.
3. **Then launch publicly.**

That existing-customer window regularly produces more sales in a few days than the first
month of public launch, because the trust already exists.

---

## The trap: building instead of distributing

The seductive failure mode after a modest first launch is to conclude you need more
products.

Usually you do not. **Usually you need more people to see the one you have.**

Before starting product two, ask honestly: is the first one selling as well as it could
to people who have not seen it yet? If you have 60 posts and 200 sales, build. If you have
60 posts and 8 sales, the problem is distribution, and a second product will sell to the
same eight people.

More products multiply distribution. They do not substitute for it.

---

## Action Items

1. **Check the threshold.** Under 50 sales, or not selling steadily? Stop here and go back
   to [[04-Market/00-Module-Overview|Module 4]].
2. **Read your support inbox.** Write down every question asked more than twice. That list
   is your product roadmap.
3. **Reopen `Course Two.md`.** Compare it against what customers actually ask for — often
   different, and the customers are right.
4. **Pick one** using the ranked list. Write its one-outcome sentence
   ([[01-Mindset/08-Committing-To-Scope]]).
5. **Email your customers** and ask what they want next. Act on the answers.
6. **Set up the bundle** once both exist.
