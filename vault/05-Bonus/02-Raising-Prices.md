# 5.2 — Raising prices without losing buyers

**10 minutes · Module 5 · [[00-Module-Overview|Back to module]]**

---

## The arithmetic people do not do

Raising your price from £47 to £79 is a 68% increase in revenue per sale.

For that to lose you money, your conversion rate would have to fall by more than 40%. In
practice a well-justified increase in this range costs you a fraction of that — often
nothing measurable, because the people who buy a £47 course and the people who buy a £79
course are largely the same people.

**You are almost certainly underpriced.** Nearly everyone is, because pricing anxiety runs
in one direction only.

---

## When to raise

Not arbitrarily. Each increase should be attached to the product genuinely being worth
more:

| Trigger | Why it justifies it |
|---------|---------------------|
| **25 sales** | Proven demand. You are no longer guessing |
| **Real testimonials** | Reduced risk for the buyer ([[04-Collecting-Testimonials]]) |
| **Added material** | More lessons, templates, updates |
| **Refund rate under 5%** | Evidence people get what they expected |
| **Selling steadily without you pushing** | Demand exceeds your current effort |

A sensible ladder:

- Launch **£47**
- 25 sales → **£59**
- 75 sales → **£79**
- 150 sales → **£97+**

Write the ladder down before you launch ([[03-Sell/01-Pricing]]). Deciding thresholds in
advance, while calm, is far easier than deciding them later while anxious about a slow
week.

---

## How to do it

### Announce it, and honour the announcement

Tell people a fortnight before:

> "The price goes to £79 on 1 November. It's £47 until then."

Three things happen, all good:

1. A burst of sales before the deadline
2. Existing customers feel they bought well
3. The increase has a reason and a date, so it does not read as opportunistic

And critically: **the price must actually change on that date.** Not changing it teaches
everyone watching that your deadlines are fiction, which costs you more than the increase
gained. Same principle as [[03-Sell/05-Honest-Urgency]].

### Grandfather existing customers

Anyone who already bought keeps what they bought, including future updates, at no extra
cost.

Say this explicitly. It removes the "will I be charged more later?" worry that makes
people hesitate, and it costs you nothing you would otherwise have had.

### Add something visible

An increase feels fairer when the product visibly improved. Add three lessons, a new
template, a checklist — then the message is *"I've added X, and the price is going up"*
rather than *"the price is going up"*.

---

## If sales drop

Give it **200 visitors** before concluding anything. Below that you are reading noise
([[04-Market/08-Reading-Analytics]]).

If conversion genuinely falls after a real sample, the usual causes in order:

1. **The page did not update to justify the new price.** Same copy, higher number. Add
   the specifics that support it.
2. **You raised too far, too fast.** £47 → £149 is a different buyer. £47 → £79 is not.
3. **Your proof did not keep pace.** Higher prices need more risk reversal — stronger
   guarantee, real testimonials, more visible contents.

**Do not immediately lower it.** Lowering is expensive: customers who paid more feel
cheated, and it signals the product was not selling. Fix the page first and give it
another sample.

---

## Never discount the base price permanently

A course permanently "60% off" is not a £97 course. It is a £39 course with a misleading
sticker — and a struck-through price nobody ever paid is a fictitious pricing claim, which
is a specific regulatory offence in most jurisdictions, not a grey area.

If you want a lower entry point, **make a smaller product**, not a permanent discount.

---

## Raising the floor instead

An alternative to raising your headline price: leave it and add a higher tier.

```
Before:  Course            £47

After:   Course            £47
         Course + feedback £149
```

Some buyers take the higher option. Your average order value rises without touching the
price that is working.

This is often the better move when your £47 conversion is strong — you capture more from
people willing to pay more, without risking the volume. See [[03-Sell/02-Tiers]], and
remember the higher tier must contain something that genuinely costs you more.

---

## The psychology worth knowing

**Higher prices can increase conversion**, within limits. Price is one of very few quality
signals available before purchase. A £7 course on a professional topic reads as low
quality — the buyer does not think "bargain", they think "what is wrong with it".

**Higher-paying customers are usually easier.** They refund less, complain less, and
complete more. Very cheap products attract people with low commitment, which produces more
support load per pound, not less.

Neither is a licence to charge arbitrarily. Both are reasons not to be afraid of £79.

---

## Action Items

1. **Check your ladder.** Have you passed a threshold without raising? Do it.
2. **Pick your next price and a date.** Put the date in your calendar.
3. **Add something visible** before the increase — lessons, a template, an update.
4. **Announce it a fortnight out**, with the date and the new price.
5. **Grandfather existing customers** explicitly, and say so.
6. **Wait 200 visitors** before judging the result.
