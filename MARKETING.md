# Marketing

The strategy in one file. The detailed 90-day plan with an exact posting schedule is
**[docs/Marketing-Playbook.md](docs/Marketing-Playbook.md)**.

---

## The shape of it

Three things, in this order of importance:

1. **Publish consistently**, on one platform, for long enough to matter
2. **Talk to everyone who responds** — this is where early sales come from
3. **Convert some of them to email**, because that is the only audience you own

Everything else is detail.

---

## The honest expectation

| Posts | What happens |
|-------|--------------|
| 1–10 | Nothing. 50–300 views. Feels pointless |
| 10–30 | Occasional 1–3k post. First comments |
| 30–60 | One does much better. You learn something |
| 60–100 | Patterns emerge |
| 100+ | Compounding, if you kept going |

**Most people stop around post 12** — precisely the stretch where nothing happens by
design. Knowing the curve is what gets you past it.

Set your evaluation point at **60 posts**, decide it now while you are calm, and do not
revisit it in week three while discouraged.

---

## Start before you finish building

The one piece of sequencing that matters: **begin posting in week two of writing, not on
launch day.**

Distribution has a long lag. Start on launch day and your launch is to nobody. Start six
weeks earlier and you have an audience, plus you have learned what they respond to — which
improves the course.

Posting while building also solves the content problem entirely. Everything you are writing
is material.

## Pick one platform

Spreading five hours across four platforms means being mediocre on all of them and
learning from none.

| Your audience | Platform |
|---------------|----------|
| Small business, trades, local services | Facebook, Instagram |
| Creative, visual, hobby | Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok |
| Under 30, broad consumer | TikTok |
| Professional, B2B, career | LinkedIn |
| Technical, niche | YouTube, Reddit |
| Anything people search for | YouTube long-form |
| Crafts, home, food, planning | Pinterest — consistently underrated |

Two worth flagging: **YouTube long-form** has the slowest start and the best end state — a
video that ranks brings viewers for years. **Pinterest** is a search engine people forget
is a search engine, pins surface for months, and competition is far lower.

Full reasoning: `vault/04-Market/02-Choosing-Your-Platform.md`.

## The content mix

| Type | Share |
|------|-------|
| Teach one thing | 50% |
| Show the problem | 20% |
| Personal — your mistakes | 15% |
| Answer a question | 10% |
| **Mention the product** | **5%** |

One post in twenty selling. That is not modesty — an account that mostly sells gets
unfollowed and stops being distributed. Your product is in your bio on all twenty posts
anyway.

## Where the ideas come from

You already wrote them:

1. **Every lesson title** → a post
2. **Every action item** → a "do this today" post
3. **Every example and mistake** → a confession post
4. **`Course Two.md`** → cut material is free content
5. **`Their Words.md`** → every question is a post
6. **Your support inbox** → every repeated question is a post

Half an hour with these gives you 40 ideas.

## The weekly rhythm

About four hours, in fixed slots:

**Sunday, 60 min** — review last week's numbers, fill next week's calendar, write five
scripts.

**Monday, 90 min** — film all five, edit all five, schedule.

**Daily, 20 min** — reply to every comment and DM.

That daily twenty minutes is not an extra. **It is where the sales come from.** A post with
400 views and six real conversations is worth more right now than one with 40,000 views and
no replies.

**Stay one week ahead.** A buffer means a bad week costs nothing visible, and gaps are
where people stop.

## The first three seconds

Watch time is the dominant ranking signal. If people scroll past in two seconds, nothing
else you did counts.

Eight hook patterns that work:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Specific mistake | "Your photos look grey. It's not the camera." |
| Contradiction | "Stop buying lighting equipment. It won't fix this." |
| Cost | "This one setting cost me three months." |
| Before/after | "Same candle. Ten seconds apart." |
| Their question | "Why do your photos look fine on your phone and terrible on the listing?" |
| Number | "Four things make a photo look amateur. Here's the first." |
| Confession | "I spent £300 on a lighting kit. It's still in the box." |
| Direct address | "If you sell candles on Etsy, this is why." |

Write ten hooks per idea. Nine will be weak — that is the expected ratio, and a discarded
sentence is far cheaper than a discarded video.

Never open with "Hi everyone, in today's video..." — three seconds spent on nothing.

## The numbers that matter

Ignore views, likes and follower count. These five:

| Number | Where | Healthy |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **3-second retention** | Platform analytics | 60%+ |
| Average watch % | Platform analytics | Higher is better |
| Shares and saves | Platform analytics | Your best guide to what to make more of |
| Link clicks | Bio link tool / dashboard | — |
| **Sales page conversion** | `/admin.html` | 1–3% cold traffic |

**Diagnose with two numbers at once:**

| Views | Conversion | Problem |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Low | — | Hooks. Fix those, nothing else |
| High | Near zero, no clicks | Wrong audience |
| High | Low, clicks happening | Sales page |
| Low | High | **Good problem.** It works, needs volume |

That last row matters: a low-traffic page converting at 4% is not failing, it is starved.
The fix is distribution, not a redesign — and people routinely rebuild a perfectly good
page instead.

## Email, from day one

Every platform can throttle you or ban you by mistake. **Your list is the only audience you
own.**

There is no follower threshold to reach first. Start with your first post.

- **Lead magnet:** a one-page checklist. Small gets used; a 40-page guide gets downloaded
  and forgotten.
- **Where to ask:** bio link, end of posts, in replies, on your sales page.
- **What to send:** one useful thing, weekly or fortnightly. One in five selling, at most.
- **Format:** plain text, 200–400 words, one idea, reply-to a real inbox.

Free tiers at MailerLite, Buttondown or Kit handle your first few thousand subscribers.

## Attribution

Tag every link:

```
yoursite.com/?utm_source=instagram
yoursite.com/?utm_source=tiktok
yoursite.com/?utm_source=newsletter
```

The dashboard then shows **sales by source** — which answers the question everyone
otherwise guesses at: which platform produces *buyers*, as opposed to views. Those are
frequently different.

## What not to do

- **Do not buy followers or engagement.** It corrupts your only feedback signal.
- **Do not automate DMs before post 60.** The manual conversations *are* the strategy early
  on; automating them removes the mechanism that produces your first customers.
- **Do not cold-DM strangers with a pitch.** Reported, muted, and it converts terribly.
- **Do not optimise posting times or hashtags.** Three to five relevant tags, a time you
  can hit consistently. The content decides the reach.
- **Do not run ads yet.** Paid traffic amplifies a converting page. Until you know your
  page converts, you are paying to learn something free traffic teaches you.
- **Do not promote in communities before participating.** Weeks of genuine answers first.

## Claims you may not make

Before writing any promotional copy, read `vault/01-Mindset/06-Ethics-And-Claims.md`.

The short version:

- **No income claims** unless you can substantiate them as typical. The safe default is to
  make none.
- **No invented testimonials.** Civil penalties per violation, and it is spotted.
- **No fake urgency.** A deadline must actually happen; a limit must actually run out.
- **You are responsible for what your affiliates claim** on your behalf.

Honest copy outperforms hype here anyway. Your reader has seen a hundred of these pages and
believes almost none of them — specificity and admitted limitations are what cut through.
